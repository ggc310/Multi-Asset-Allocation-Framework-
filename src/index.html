                                                          Purpose

                                                          This is not a systematic investment strategy. The dashboard exists to synthesize the weight of the evidence — valuation, fundamentals, trend, sentiment, macro, earnings calls and the record of each signal — so that a person can make better-informed decisions. It is intentionally flexible: every model, weight and threshold can be changed, and no output is meant to be followed mechanically.

                                                          It is built to support investor judgment, not to replace it. Read the scores, model calls and commentary as inputs to a human decision-making process, alongside context the data cannot capture.

                                                          The dashboard covers a global multi-asset portfolio: how much to hold in equities against bonds, how to tilt the bond portfolio against the US Aggregate, which diversifiers and currencies are in a supportive state, and, inside equities, which regions, styles, sectors and countries look better or worse supported. The decisions are framed over 12 to 36 months; the Today page and the daily commentary cover what moves in between.

                                                          The equity part is a relative-opportunity screen for the building blocks of a global equity allocation, from core indexes down to sectors, industries and single countries. It asks one question: over the next 12 to 36 months, which parts of the equity opportunity set look better or worse supported than the rest? It ranks; it does not forecast returns. Use it to decide where to spend research time and to challenge existing tilts, not as a trading signal.

                                                          Every position, in every asset class, is held through a liquid US-listed ETF. Equity fundamentals come from looking through each ETF to the companies it holds; bond, real-asset and currency views come from prices, yields, distributions and the macro record. Prices are live.

Opening it during the day gives a current read on prices and everything built from them; the models and the fundamental view behind the rankings move once a week; the commentary is written on its own schedule.

                                                          How to use it

                                                          • Today is the daily read: market moves, headlines from major financial outlets, and the economic and earnings calendar with filters.
                                                          • Executive summary is the decision page. The decision summary at the top gives each view and its size, what changed since the previous saved review, the observations behind it, how strong the evidence is, what dominates the resulting portfolio and what would reverse each call. Below it: the combined model portfolio and its risk, what markets have done over the period you pick, what is changing, the risks to the view and how much to trust each model.
                                                          • Commentary holds the written pieces, from The Day Ahead to the Quarter in Review.
                                                          • Decide holds the forward-looking models. Allocation: stocks vs bonds, fixed income relative value, real assets, currencies and the model lab. Equities: the scorecard that ranks 62 equity sleeves, relative trends (each dimension’s score against its relative price), country and style allocation with the portfolio check, cash deployment and scenarios.
                                                          • Markets looks back: multi-asset performance across 36 assets from one day to twenty years; the macro backdrop; Compare & correlate for any assets across classes; equity pulse, short-term returns, long-run returns, time series, visuals and breadth; the Treasury curve and bond markets; inflation, real yields and credit; currencies and the pair explorer.
                                                          • Companies holds the earnings-call analysis and the company profiles for the largest holdings.
                                                          • Evidence holds the model scorecard, each model’s backtest, the equity live record and score history, and this page.
                                                          • The universe filter and equity model settings appear only on the equity pages, where they apply. Click any table heading to sort; ranked charts have their own sort control; every time-series chart has a time-frame control.

                                                          Decision summary

                                                          • What it shows. One row per view (stocks vs bonds, duration, bond sectors, equity regions, equity style and size, diversifiers, the dollar): the tilt and its size, the evidence grade, the main observation behind it and the first condition that would reverse it. Opening a row shows every driver, the context the models do not use, what supports and weakens the call, the reversal levels and the coverage warnings.
                                                          • Reversal levels. For stocks vs bonds they are solved from the live score’s own arithmetic: the level of one input, others held where they are, at which the equity weight would go to neutral or to the mirror-image underweight. For bond levers they are the yield gaps at which a position starts and reaches full size.
                                                          • Evidence grade. A fixed rubric, applied the same way to every view: +1 if every live signal predicted in both halves of the record (before 2019 and from 2020), −1 if one has turned negative since 2020; +1 if a live signal has a t-stat of 1.5 or more since 2020; +1 if the backtest’s 90% range is above zero, −1 if the timing itself lost money; +1 if the live inputs agree, −1 if they offset. Strong is 3 or more, Moderate 2, Mixed 1, Weak 0 or less.
                                                          • What changed. Each weekly review saves its calls and the observations behind them. The page compares the latest review with the previous saved one using those saved values; recorded market series such as Treasury yields are read at the earlier review’s date. When there is no earlier review, the page says so instead of reconstructing one from backtest history.
                                                          • What dominates the portfolio. The equity/bond split against the reference, expected volatility and tracking error, the share of active risk by decision, exposure to equities, rates and the dollar from the risk model, the largest funds and look-through companies, and the current stock–bond correlation.

                                                          Stocks vs bonds model

                                                          • What it decides. The equity weight in a portfolio of global equities (ACWI) and US bonds (AGG), around a 60/40 neutral and within 45% to 75%: 60% plus 15 points times the blended score.
                                                          • Four pillars, weighted 50/0/25/25. Valuation 50%, momentum 0%, macro 25%, risk 25%. A signal carries weight only if it passes the promotion rule (see Macro, regimes, positioning); a pillar with no passing signal drops out and the others are reweighted.
                                                          • Live now. Valuation: the bond term premium, read from the 2s10s slope against its history (a steep curve pays bond holders more, so it counts against equities). Macro: global growth surprises. These two carry the whole score; the risk pillar has no passing signal.
                                                          • Shown, not used. The equity risk premium (the point-in-time S&P 500 cycle-adjusted earnings yield, built from the index’s members and earnings as filed, less the 10-year Treasury yield minus trailing CPI inflation); high yield against duration-matched Treasuries over six months; the US growth nowcast; three-month equity volatility against its three-year median; S&P 500 futures positioning; and momentum, equities against bonds over 12 months and against their 10-month average. Each failed the promotion rule and is displayed as context.
                                                          • Real yield. The models use one definition everywhere: the 10-year Treasury yield less trailing CPI inflation, lagged a month. The market’s TIPS real yield is shown separately on the inflation and credit page and on the decision summary.
                                                          • Scoring. Each input is a z-score against its own history up to that month, clipped at two standard deviations and squashed to −1 to +1. Nothing uses data that was not available at the time.
                                                          • Backtest. Monthly from June 2016, when global growth surprises begin: the weight set at each month-end earns the next month’s ACWI and AGG returns. Compared with a static 60/40, with a block-bootstrap 90% range, months ahead, turnover and each signal’s rank correlation with the following one and twelve months. The excess is split into the average tilt and the timing around it.

                                                          Fixed income relative value model

                                                          • What it decides. Tilts against the US Aggregate that keep risk close to the benchmark’s: hold AGG and apply duration-aware swaps between bond ETFs.
                                                          • Duration (±1.5 years, long Treasuries against short): longer when real yields are high and the curve is steep against their history.
                                                          • Investment-grade credit (±15%, LQD against IEF) and agency MBS (±10%, MBB against IEF): their spread signals did not pass the promotion rule, so both are held at the Aggregate’s weight until one does.
                                                          • High yield (up to 10%, HYG against IEI) and EM dollar debt (up to 8%, EMB against IEF): added only when cheap, since the Agg holds none.
                                                          • TIPS (up to 10%, TIP against IEI): when inflation has been rising over six months.
                                                          • EM local currency (up to 5%, EMLC against short Treasuries): after a strong six-month dollar run, which leaves EM currencies cheap. Local yields and the currency model are shown as evidence only.
                                                          • Signals follow the same z-score method and promotion rule as the stocks vs bonds model. Spreads are distribution yields over Treasury ETFs of similar duration, a proxy for option-adjusted spreads, which FMP does not carry.
                                                          • Backtest. Monthly from December 2010, when at least five levers have enough history: the Agg plus each lever’s position times the return of its long leg less its short leg. Reported against the Agg: excess return with a 90% range, tracking error, information ratio, months ahead, worst run behind, and each lever’s contribution, hit rate and rank correlation.
                                                          • Design choice. An earlier version also used momentum and risk-off (VIX) signals. Over this period they reduced returns at the 12-month horizon, so the model now buys value only. That choice was made after seeing results; treat the backtest as an upper bound.

                                                          Model portfolio and risk

                                                          • Combination. Equities get the policy neutral plus the band times the stocks vs bonds score; the equity share is first split into country funds by tilting ACWI’s country weights with the equity scores (the Equity allocation page settings), with countries that have no fund held through ACWX. Then up to the sleeve budget (20% of equities by default, at most 5% in any one sleeve) moves into the best-scoring sleeves anywhere in the equity universe: sectors, industries, US and international styles and sizes, factors and regional funds. A sleeve qualifies when its composite is more than the allocation band (at least 5 points) above 50; weights follow how far above, up to ten sleeves, skipping any that moves almost one-for-one (correlation above 0.92) with a better-scoring pick. Each is funded pro rata from the broad and country funds of its own region, so region weights, and the region look-through, are unchanged. Negative views are expressed by not holding a sleeve; nothing is sold short. Bonds are the Agg plus the fixed income pair trades. An optional diversifier sleeve, funded pro rata, holds the real assets that score above zero.
                                                          • Reference. The neutral weight in ACWI, the rest in AGG. The table shows ACWI split into the same country funds, so active weights read country by country.
                                                          • Risk. A covariance matrix of every fund the portfolio can hold (78: all 62 equity sleeves plus the bond, real-asset and dollar funds) from the last five years of monthly returns. The active risk is split into the stocks vs bonds call, the country tilts, the sleeve tilts, holding country funds instead of ACWI (an implementation choice), the fixed income tilts and the diversifiers. Tracking error is the volatility of the active weights; each decision’s contribution is its covariance with the total active position divided by the tracking error, so contributions add to the total.
                                                          • Scenarios. Each fund’s sensitivity to global equities, the 10-year yield, the dollar and oil comes from a five-year monthly regression; a shock is applied through those sensitivities to every fund and to the portfolio and reference.

                                                          Real assets and commodities

                                                          • Assets. Gold (GLD), broad commodities (DBC), US REITs (VNQ) and global infrastructure (IGF), judged as diversifiers for a 60/40 portfolio.
                                                          • Signals. Trend (the 12-month return and the price against its 10-month average) is live for gold and broad commodities; the inflation nowcast is live for commodities. Diversification, the three-year correlation with 60/40 (good below 0.5), is kept for all four as a risk property, not a forecast. Value (the real 10-year yield for gold, the REIT yield over the 10-year) and positioning failed the promotion rule and are shown as evidence. Each signal is scaled to −1 to +1 and averaged.
                                                          • Use in the portfolio. The diversifier sleeve on the model portfolio is 0% unless you set it; when used, it holds the assets that score above zero in proportion to their scores.
                                                          • Evidence. The 12-month rank correlation of the score with the next year’s return over 60/40 since 2009 is small for every asset; read the scores as a guide to which diversifier is in a supportive state, not as a forecast.

                                                          Currencies

                                                          • Coverage. Six developed and six emerging currencies against the dollar, quoted as dollars per unit so a rise means the currency gained. Weekly history back to 2005; daily returns are live.
                                                          • Valuation. Against the currency’s own 10-year average, and against the World Bank’s purchasing-power-parity rate (positive means cheap). PPP gaps are large and persistent for emerging currencies; compare them with their own history, not with zero.
                                                          • Hedge ratio. For dollar-based holders of developed-market equities, from the currency model: 50%, moving toward 0% when it expects the currency to gain and toward 100% when it expects it to fall. Where the model has no view, a rule: 50%, plus 25 points if the currency is more than 5% above its 10-year average, minus 25 if more than 5% below, plus 25 if it fell more than 2% over 12 months, minus 25 if it rose more than 2%, within 0% to 100%. A rule of thumb, not a backtested model.

                                                          Live records, alerts and the committee pack

                                                          • Live records. Each Saturday the stocks vs bonds weight, the fixed income tilts and their pair funds, the real asset scores, the currency views, the regime, the equity preferences and the observations behind them are saved. Each call is held until the next one and measured with daily fund prices; the Model scorecard page compounds them.
                                                          • Running count. Every commentary piece ends with the live track record to its data date: stocks vs bonds against a 60/40, fixed income tilts against the Aggregate, and the favored minus the avoided equity sleeves, since the first saved call and for the latest session. A record this short says little about skill; it is shown so the count is honest from the start.
                                                          • Alerts. The scheduled rebuild emails a short note when a call changes materially: the equity weight by 3 points or more, a fixed income tilt changing sign or moving 5 points (half a year for duration), a real asset score crossing zero, or a change in the top or bottom equity sleeves.
                                                          • Committee pack. A PDF of the weekly note, the model calls and the main charts, rebuilt each Saturday and linked from the Executive summary.

                                                          Short term and what’s changing

                                                          • Periods. 1D is today’s change against the prior close. WTD runs from the last close before Monday. 1W is five trading days, MTD from the last close of the prior month, 1M is 21 trading days. All are total returns from dividend-adjusted prices ending at the live price. QTD runs from the last close of the prior quarter, 3M and 6M are 63 and 126 trading days, 1Y is 252. 2Y comes from daily prices; 3Y to 20Y from weekly prices since 2005. From 2Y on, returns are annualized, and funds without the full history are left out of that period. “vs global equities” shows each return relative to ACWI.
                                                          • Unusual moves are measured in standard deviations of the sleeve’s own daily returns over the past three months, scaled to the period. Beyond two is flagged.
                                                          • What’s changing is rule-based. It flags style, size and region pairs whose one-month relative return reverses their 12-month direction; 12-month leaders that lose ground and laggards that rebound; crosses of the 200-day average; new 52-week highs and lows; outsized weekly moves; shifts in breadth, return dispersion and volatility; rates, dollar, credit, oil and gold moving against their six-month trend; sleeves the data favors that the market is selling and the reverse; and regime labels that changed over three months. Items are ordered by how far they sit from normal.
                                                          • Short-term returns are context, not inputs. The ranking uses 12-month momentum and trend, not the last week.

                                                          How the commentary is written

                                                          • Commentary tab. Written by Claude scheduled tasks from FMP and FRED data, in a house voice: The Day Ahead before each trading day’s open, Day in Review after its close, Week in Review on Fridays, The Week Ahead on Sundays, Month and Quarter in Review after each period’s last close, and earnings-call takeaways in busy reporting weeks. Every number is checked against the data pulled before publishing; figures that cannot be confirmed are cut. The weekly, monthly and quarterly pieces are also emailed.
                                                          • Page text. Every other sentence on the dashboard (the headline, the decision summary, the scorecard, category notes, What’s changing and the weekly note) is generated from the numbers, by rule, each time the page opens. Nothing is edited by hand.
                                                          • For each equity sleeve, a metric is cited as support when it sits in the most favorable quarter of the universe, and as a weakness when it sits in the least favorable quarter. Cycle-adjusted P/E is judged against the sleeve’s own history instead, using the top and bottom 30% of its range. The strongest two or three are quoted with their values.
                                                          • Category notes name the top and bottom sleeves under the current settings, the pillar that most separates them from the rest of the category, the forward P/E range and recent returns. A line marked Against the view flags recent performance that contradicts the ranking.
                                                          • Sentiment evidence follows the sentiment setting: read contrarian, outflows and low Buy shares count in a sleeve’s favor and crowded positioning against it.
                                                          • Critical points are in bold.

                                                          Bond markets

                                                          • Coverage. 27 bond ETFs: Treasuries by maturity and TIPS; the US aggregate; agency MBS, AAA CLOs and municipals; investment-grade corporates by rating and maturity, and floating-rate notes; BB and broad high yield, fallen angels and senior loans; EM dollar and local-currency debt and hedged international bonds; preferreds and convertibles.
                                                          • Curve. FMP Treasury yields, live, from one month to 30 years, compared with one day, one week, one month, three months and one year earlier. The curve is described as bull or bear (yields falling or rising) and steepening or flattening, from the 2-year and 10-year moves. Month-end history since 2005 comes from the weekly rebuild.
                                                          • Returns. Dividend-adjusted total returns to the live price, over the same periods as every other asset; 3 to 20 years are annualized from daily history since 2005. Returns against duration-matched Treasuries subtract the return of Treasury ETFs interpolated to the same empirical duration.
                                                          • Yield. Trailing 12-month distributions over today’s price, and the latest distribution annualized. These are distribution yields, not yields to maturity: they lag market yields as coupons reset, they include TIPS inflation accruals, and municipal yields are before tax.
                                                          • Duration and equity beta. Weekly returns over three years are regressed on the weekly change in the Treasury yield at the maturity that fits best and on US equity returns. The yield coefficient is an empirical duration; the equity coefficient shows how much credit behaves like stocks. Floating-rate and loan funds show near-zero duration by construction.
                                                          • Yield over Treasuries. A fund’s trailing yield less the trailing yield of Treasury ETFs interpolated to the same duration. Using distribution yields on both sides cancels most of the coupon lag. It is a spread proxy, not an option-adjusted spread.
                                                          • Holdings-level bond data (ratings mix, maturities) is not available from FMP, so fund-level credit measures come from prices and distributions. Index option-adjusted spreads by rating come from FRED, on the inflation, real yields and credit page.

                                                          Inflation, real yields and credit

                                                          • Inflation and real yields. Treasury breakevens (5-year, 10-year and the 5-year, 5-year forward) and TIPS real yields (5, 10 and 30 years) from FRED, daily since 2003, with the 10-year yield split into its real yield and breakeven.
                                                          • Credit. ICE BofA option-adjusted spreads and total-return indexes for investment grade and high yield, and by rating from AAA to CCC, from FRED. FRED carries only the last three years of these series, so spread history and returns by rating start about three years back.
                                                          • Rebuilt with each full price refresh and every Saturday.

                                                          Compare & correlate

                                                          • Coverage. Equity sleeves, bond funds, real assets, commodity futures, the dollar index and twelve currencies, bitcoin and ether, the VIX, and Treasury yields, real yields, breakevens and credit spreads, in one list.
                                                          • History. Weekly closes from 2005–07 for the non-equity assets, joined to the daily history of the last few years and scaled where they meet. Rates and spreads are levels in percent from FRED.
                                                          • Views. Growth of 100, relative to a benchmark, drawdown, rolling volatility, rolling correlation and levels; statistics, a correlation matrix (daily, weekly or monthly returns) and calendar-year returns follow the time frame you pick. Yields and spreads are compared in basis-point changes, not returns.

                                                          Companies

                                                          • Earnings calls. Every S&P 500 earnings call since 2014, scored for tone, guidance and topics; seasons are calendar quarters with at least 25 calls, and changes compare each company with its own call a year earlier. Tone readings roll up to sectors and sleeves and are tested against later returns like any other signal.
                                                          • Company profile. The largest holdings across the dashboard’s funds (about 300), with price history against the market and the sector, revenue and earnings with estimates, margins, cash flow, the balance sheet, the earnings record, key metrics, call tone, which funds hold it, and peers. In the Claude version any ticker can be looked up live.

                                                          Equity scorecard

                                                          The sections from here to the metric reference describe the equity scorecard, which ranks the 62 equity sleeves and feeds the equity country tilts in the model portfolio.

                                                          The four pillars

                                                          Each pillar answers a different question, and they are chosen so that some pull against each other. A sleeve that scores well on all four is rare; most of the insight comes from where they disagree.

                                                          • Fundamentals, made of quality and growth, asks whether the underlying businesses are good and getting better. Quality covers profitability, cash backing of earnings, leverage and how predictable earnings are. Growth covers consensus earnings and revenue growth over the current and next fiscal year, how broad that growth is across holdings, and, once 13 weeks of weekly snapshots exist, how consensus estimates have been revised.
                                                          • Valuation asks what you are paying for those fundamentals. It is the slowest-acting pillar: a weak guide to the next few months but one of the better guides to multi-year returns. It deliberately pulls against fundamentals, because the best businesses are rarely the cheapest.
                                                          • Technicals asks whether the market agrees. It rewards a steady, intact uptrend, because medium-term momentum tends to persist and because a trend filter keeps the screen from favoring markets that are cheap because they are still falling.
                                                          • Sentiment asks how crowded the trade is, from analyst ratings and targets and from fund flows. By default it is read contrarian, so it pulls against technicals: a strong trend that everyone already owns scores lower than one the crowd has not yet joined. The next section explains why.

                                                          How sentiment is read

                                                          Sentiment measures positioning: how much optimism is already in the price. It has three inputs: how many analysts rate the holdings a buy, how far their price targets sit above today's prices, and how much money has flowed into the fund over six months (growth in the ETF's shares outstanding). Price breadth and RSI used to sit here too. Read contrarian, they pointed the wrong way in the backtest, because they are really momentum measures, so they now appear only on the Breadth tab. The test results are in the table below.

                                                          Contrarian (default). Over 12 to 36 months, extremes in positioning tend to reverse. When nearly every analyst rates a market a buy and most of its stocks are at highs, good news is priced in, expectations are hard to beat and there are few marginal buyers left. When sentiment is washed out, the opposite holds. In this mode, crowded optimism lowers the score and pessimism raises it.

                                                          Confirming. Every sentiment metric flips, so optimism and broad participation raise the score. This suits shorter horizons or a regime where you expect trends to keep running. In this mode sentiment largely repeats what technicals already measures, so the composite leans harder toward momentum. Because scores are percentile ranks, a sleeve's confirming sentiment score is exactly 100 minus its contrarian score. Each mode has its own composite and group rank, and the switch in the equity settings changes every equity view on the page.

                                                          Reading technicals and contrarian sentiment together gives four setups:

                                                          • Strong trend, washed-out sentiment: a recovery the crowd has not joined yet. Usually the most attractive combination.
                                                          • Strong trend, crowded sentiment: a mature trend. It can keep going, but expect less upside and sharper pullbacks.
                                                          • Weak trend, washed-out sentiment: capitulation. A contrarian opportunity if fundamentals and valuation support it, though it usually pays to wait for the trend to turn.
                                                          • Weak trend, crowded sentiment: optimism the market is not confirming. The weakest setup.

                                                          Two metrics that used to sit in sentiment now live in fundamentals, because they describe earnings rather than positioning. Analyst agreement on this year's EPS is part of quality, as earnings visibility. The share of holdings with rising next-year EPS is part of growth, as growth breadth.

                                                          How fund-level numbers are built

                                                          • Holdings. Each ETF is looked through to its largest holdings, enough to cover most of its weight. Concentrated funds are covered almost completely. Broad, equal-weight and small-cap funds hold hundreds or thousands of names, so coverage is lower; each sleeve's coverage is shown under its holdings table and in the universe table below. Weights are re-scaled across the holdings that have data for each metric.
                                                          • Valuation multiples are aggregated the way index providers do it: the weighted average of each company's earnings, book, EBITDA or cash-flow yield is inverted back into a multiple. This keeps a handful of very expensive or loss-making names from dominating the result.
                                                          • Growth is earnings-weighted: it is the growth of the fund's aggregate earnings, not an average of company growth rates, so a small company tripling its earnings from a low base does not swamp the result. Revenue growth is weighted the same way, by sales.
                                                          • Forward P/E uses a next-twelve-months EPS that blends the current and next fiscal year by the time left in the current one. Estimates are in each company's reporting currency, so forward multiples are scaled from FMP's trailing P/E, which keeps currencies consistent. Companies whose estimates don't reconcile with their trailing EPS are left out of growth and forward P/E.
                                                          • Quality uses weighted medians, which are robust to outliers. Banks and insurers are excluded from cash conversion, return on invested capital, net debt to EBITDA, EV to EBITDA and free cash flow yield, where their accounts do not compare with other companies.
                                                          • Coverage rule. A fund-level metric is shown only when holdings worth at least a quarter of the fund have usable data. Otherwise it is left blank and the pillar is averaged from the metrics that remain.
                                                          • Live repricing. Fundamentals are a snapshot, but valuation multiples move with each ETF's price since the snapshot date, so a sell-off makes a sleeve cheaper on the page the same day.

                                                          How scores are built

                                                          • Percentile ranks. Each metric is ranked across the whole universe and expressed as a percentile from 0 to 100, oriented so that higher is always better under the current reading. Ranks rather than raw values keep one extreme number from dominating and put metrics on a common scale.
                                                          • Pillars. A pillar is the simple average of its metrics' percentiles. Fundamentals averages quality and growth.
                                                          • Composite. The equal-weight average of fundamentals, valuation, technicals and sentiment.
                                                          • Valuation: cycle-adjusted P/E against the sleeve's own history. By default the valuation pillar asks one question: is this sleeve's price high or low relative to its average earnings over the last five years, compared with its own record since 2011? Each sleeve's position in its own range is then ranked across the universe. This is the logic of Shiller's CAPE: it ignores sector and country mix, which dominate any comparison of one sleeve's multiple with another's, and it does not mistake peak-cycle earnings for cheapness.
                                                          • Why this default. Five ways of measuring valuation were tested. Every method that compares multiples across sleeves, or uses ordinary trailing multiples, pointed the wrong way in a decade led by US growth. The cycle-adjusted own-history read was the only one with a positive record (12-month rank correlation +0.08, top fifth ahead of the bottom fifth by about 6% over the next year), in the US and in developed markets abroad. It needs three years of earnings and two years of its own record, so its test runs only from late 2019 and is not statistically strong. The Valuation control switches to the other four: within category, all multiples against own history, across the universe, or a blend.
                                                          • Still relative. The final score is always a rank, so if everything is expensive, something still scores 100.
                                                          • Cycle-adjusted P/E. Valuation includes price over the sleeve's average trailing earnings of the last five years, from its rebuilt monthly history. Ordinary P/Es look cheapest at the top of an earnings cycle; the five-year average largely removes that trap.
                                                          • Conviction. Every sleeve is also ranked under all 70 combinations of the seven presets, two sentiment readings and five valuation bases. Conviction is how often it lands in the top or bottom fifth. The Bottom line on the Scoreboard lists the sleeves that do so in at least 60% of settings, flags leaders whose margins sit at the top of their own range or whose data failed a check, and states what the current setting has meant historically.
                                                          • Group rank. Narrow industries and broad indexes compete in the same ranking. The In group column, and the universe filter, let you compare like with like.

                                                          • Within-category test. Picking the top fifth inside each category, which neutralizes region and style bets, still beat the universe in the backtest, so the edge is not only a region or style call.

                                                          Weight presets and signals

                                                          The composite blends the four pillars with one of seven preset weightings, or your own. Each preset is a different investment stance:

                                                          • Equal weight (25% each): no stance. The default.
                                                          • Value tilt (fundamentals 30, valuation 40, technicals 15, sentiment 15): leans on starting valuation, the slowest but most persistent driver of multi-year returns.
                                                          • Momentum (15, 15, 50, 20): follows the trend. Suits shorter holding periods.
                                                          • Quality (45, 25, 15, 15): favors durable, growing businesses at a reasonable price.
                                                          • Contrarian (20, 35, 10, 35): buys cheap, unloved markets. Pairs naturally with the contrarian sentiment reading.
                                                          • Long horizon, 12 to 36 months (30, 35, 20, 15): the blend most consistent with this tool's horizon.
                                                          • Fitted to 6, 12 or 36 months: weights from each pillar's rank correlation with returns at that horizon, shrunk halfway to equal; see the Backtest tab for the current figures.
                                                          • Backtest-informed (50, 10, 20, 20): leans on quality, the one pillar with a consistently positive record in the backtest, and keeps only a small valuation weight. It held up under both sentiment readings and both holdings bases, but its weights were chosen after seeing the results, so treat its backtest as flattering.
                                                          • Custom: enter any four weights; they are rescaled to sum to 100%.

                                                          When a sleeve is missing a pillar, its composite is the weighted average of the pillars it has. Signals follow the composite: at or above the Overweight threshold reads Overweight, at or below the Underweight threshold reads Underweight, and anything between is Neutral. Both thresholds are adjustable above the tabs.

                                                          Deployment rules

                                                          • Sleeves are ranked by composite under the current weights, sentiment reading and universe filter.
                                                          • A sleeve is eligible when its composite clears the minimum and, if required, it has both a fundamentals and a valuation score. Global blends (ACWI, ACWX) can be excluded, since they overlap with everything else.
                                                          • Picks are taken in rank order, skipping any sleeve whose 36-month return correlation with an earlier pick is above the maximum. This keeps the picks from being one bet three times.
                                                          • The amount is split in proportion to how far each pick's composite sits above 50, so stronger conviction gets more money.
                                                          • The avoid list is the lowest composites in the current universe. None of this is advice; it is a disciplined way to turn the screen into a short list.

                                                          Visuals, trends and allocation

                                                          • Four-box matrix: any two pillars against each other, split at the universe midpoint of 50, with the members of each box listed.
                                                          • Metric explorer: any two metrics or pillars across the sleeves, with a least-squares line, R² and correlation. The strongest-relationships table ranks every pair of metrics by R², optionally only pairs from different pillars, which shows which signals are telling you the same thing.
                                                          • Relative strength: a sleeve's month-end price divided by a benchmark's, rebased to 100. A rising line means it is outperforming.
                                                          • Correlation: correlation of monthly dividend-adjusted returns over the latest 36 months. Below about 0.5 is a meaningful diversifier.
                                                          • Equities against bonds: the cycle-adjusted earnings yield of the US, global, developed ex-US and emerging sleeves less the US real 10-year yield (10-year Treasury less trailing CPI inflation), with its range since 2011. The regime table labels each month by rates, curve, dollar, credit, inflation and equity trend, and shows how each pillar ranked sleeves in months like each state.
                                                          • Portfolio check: paste holdings and weights to see the portfolio's weighted scores, the share of weight in each signal, holdings ranked low under most settings, sleeves you could add that are in the top fifth under most settings and least correlated with what you hold, and signal-tilted weights using the tilt settings below.
                                                          • Country tilts: each country's ACWI weight is scaled by one plus the maximum tilt times its score's distance from 50 (as a share of 50), then all weights are rescaled to sum to 100%. Scores inside the neutral band count as 50. Countries without a sleeve stay neutral.
                                                          • Pair preferences: the score gap between two sleeves; a gap inside the neutral band reads as neutral.

                                                          Score history

                                                          Each time the fundamentals snapshot is rebuilt, every sleeve's pillar scores and composites are saved with the date. The History tab compares today's live scores with any saved snapshot, under the same weights and sentiment reading, and labels each sleeve improving, fading or stable using the band you set. Live scores move every day with prices; the fundamentals underneath change only when the snapshot is rebuilt.

                                                          Breadth and concentration

                                                          • Breadth uses live quotes on every holding in each fund's look-through: the weighted share above its own 50- and 200-day averages, within 5% of its 52-week high or low, and up or down today. Narrow breadth under a rising price means a handful of large names is carrying the fund.
                                                          • Concentration reports the largest holding, the top-10 weight and the effective number of holdings (one over the sum of squared weights, with holdings beyond the look-through assumed equally weighted), plus the largest sector and country and the effective number of sectors.
                                                          • Sector exposure is each fund's look-through sector mix, as a share of the holdings covered.

                                                          Returns and time series

                                                          • Historical returns come from dividend-adjusted weekly closes since 2005, ending at today's live price. Annualized figures need the full window of history; younger funds show a dash.
                                                          • Calendar-year and monthly returns use the last weekly close of each year or month.
                                                          • The time series explorer shows monthly look-through multiples (trailing P/E, price to book, EV to EBITDA, free cash flow and dividend yields), quality metrics, breadth and rebuilt scores for each sleeve since 2011; the median of each across a group or region; and quarterly multiples for each fund's 25 largest holdings. Multiples are built from quarterly financials, summed over four quarters and used 60 days after each quarter ends, divided by the price at each month-end.
                                                          • Against its own history, a single series is shown with its average and a band of one standard deviation, and the table gives today's percentile within its own range. This answers the question the cross-sectional scores cannot: is a sleeve cheap compared with its own past?
                                                          • Each month uses the holdings the fund had filed by then, so the history describes the fund as it actually was. Before a fund's first filing in FMP (late 2019 to late 2021), its earliest filing stands in.

                                                          Equity backtest

                                                          • How much to trust it. Confidence ranges come from a block bootstrap that resamples whole 12-month stretches of history, so serial correlation is respected. The selection check compares the default setting with all 70 tested: the median setting is the fairer forecast, because the default was chosen after seeing the results.
                                                          • Live track record. Every weekly snapshot's top and bottom fifth is held until the next snapshot, on the History tab. It is the only evidence the model could not have been tuned to, and it needs a year or more before it means much.
                                                          • At every month-end from January 2011 the model is rebuilt using only data available then: each fund's holdings as filed with the SEC, technicals from prices, quality and valuation from quarterly financials lagged 60 days, and sentiment from six-month fund flows lagged three months. Valuation is the cycle-adjusted P/E against each sleeve's own history, as in the live model. That read needs five years of earnings history (at least three) and two years of its own record, so it begins in late 2019; before then, composites are built from the other three pillars. Each pillar is percentile-ranked across the sleeves that existed at that date.
                                                          • Growth and estimate inputs cannot be rebuilt (FMP keeps no history of consensus estimates or targets), so the backtested fundamentals pillar is quality only and sentiment uses flows alone. Analyst rating history from 2019 was used to test rating drift, which did not help at 12 to 36 months.
                                                          • Three valuation methods are compared side by side, and every test is repeated with today's holdings and over the fully filed window, so you can see how much each result depends on those choices.
                                                          • Portfolios are held for the following month and rebalanced monthly, equally weighted, with no costs. The 12-month-hold variant averages the last twelve monthly cohorts, matching the model's horizon. The deployment rule applies the same picks logic as the Deploy tab.
                                                          • The rank correlation between each score and subsequent returns at horizons from one to 36 months shows whether the signal has pointed the right way, and over what horizon. Returns by score fifth show whether the relationship is steady from the bottom to the top of the ranking.
                                                          • Treat the results as fifteen years of evidence, dominated by US large-cap growth. A signal that failed in this regime can still be sound, and one that worked can still be luck.

                                                          SleeveCheckLevelDetail

                                                          Macro record, regimes, positioning and currencies

                                                          • One record of the backdrop. Rebuilt every Saturday and read by every model, narrative and backtest table. It replaces the separate backdrop labels used before.
                                                          • Surprise indices. For each release in FMP’s economic calendar with a consensus forecast (about 170,000 since 2014), the surprise is the actual less the forecast, scaled by that release’s own past surprises and signed so good news is positive (unemployment and jobless claims reversed). Releases are sorted into growth and inflation, and averaged by region with a 30-day half-life over 120 days. Global is a GDP-weighted blend.
                                                          • Three axes. Growth: industrial production, payrolls, unemployment, jobless claims, real retail sales, housing starts, consumer sentiment, and US and global growth surprises. Inflation: CPI acceleration and trend, commodities, import prices, wages, consumer inflation expectations, and inflation surprises. Financial conditions: high yield against Treasuries, the VIX, the dollar, the equity trend, mortgage rates and the 10-year yield. Each input is scaled against its own history to that month; monthly data are lagged a month for publication.
                                                          • Regimes. Each axis is on one side or the other, with a probability from a normal curve; two axes give four states and three give eight. History runs from 2001.
                                                          • Positioning. Speculators’ net position in 23 futures markets as a share of open interest, against its own past three years, from the CFTC reports in FMP. Read as contrarian.
                                                          • Currency models. Cyclical: the policy-rate gap and its six-month change, the growth-surprise gap, the 12-1 month trend, positioning (contrarian) and sensitivity to financial conditions (havens gain as conditions tighten). Long-term: the real exchange rate against its 10-year average and the real policy-rate gap. Purchasing-power parity is shown but left out of the score, since its factors are estimated after the fact. Equal weights within each, as specified before testing.
                                                          • Promotion rule. Every signal, the original ones included, carries weight only if its 12-month rank correlation with what it predicts is at least 0.05 on signals dated to 2018 and no worse than −0.05 from 2020. Signals that fail stay visible as evidence. Correlations on overlapping 12-month windows overstate the evidence, so each comes with a t-stat that counts only one independent observation a year; few are above 2. Equity valuation uses the point-in-time S&P 500 equity risk premium, built from the index’s members on each date and earnings as filed. The registry below lists every signal and its status.

                                                          Limitations

                                                          • FMP carries analyst ratings and price targets mainly for US-listed companies, so for international sleeves, sentiment rests mostly on fund flows.
                                                          • Small-cap and equal-weight look-throughs cover a minority of each fund, so their fundamentals describe the larger holdings more than the whole fund.
                                                          • Consensus estimates can be stale or thin for smaller and emerging-market companies. Memory makers dominate Korea and a large part of EM and Taiwan; their consensus EPS is rising very quickly, which makes those sleeves look cheap on forward P/E and fast-growing.
                                                          • Equity scores are cross-sectional: they rank sleeves against each other and say nothing about whether equities as a whole are attractive. That question belongs to the stocks vs bonds model.
                                                          • The allocation models rest on few live signals (two for stocks vs bonds), with t-stats mostly below 2 and backtests that start in 2010–16. Treat their calls as one input, sized modestly.
                                                          • Bond spreads in the fixed income model are distribution-yield proxies, not option-adjusted spreads, and FRED’s ICE BofA history covers only three years.
                                                          • Sector mix drives many fundamental metrics, such as margins, P/B and leverage. Comparing within a group is often more informative than across the whole universe.
